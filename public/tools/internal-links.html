Internal linking guide

What Are Internal Links?

Internal links connect one page on your website to another page on the same website. They help visitors discover related information, navigate important sections and understand how topics connect. They can also help search engines find pages and understand relationships between content.

Good internal linking starts with usefulness. Link when a destination provides context, a next step or a deeper explanation that helps the reader.

How to Find Internal Linking Opportunities

1. Identify important pages

List key service pages, product pages, guides and supporting articles. Decide which pages are most useful to visitors and which topics need stronger connections.

2. Find relevant contextual links

Review pages that mention a topic covered in more depth elsewhere on your site. Add a link where it naturally supports the sentence and gives readers a useful next step.

3. Write descriptive anchor text

Use concise wording that communicates what visitors can expect on the destination page. Avoid vague anchors when a more specific phrase would make the link clearer, and do not force exact-match keywords into every link.

4. Check the destination and user journey

Verify that links point to the intended live URL and that the destination answers the question implied by the anchor text. Check for broken links, redirects and unnecessary navigation loops.

5. Review important pages periodically

When publishing a new article or updating a page, look for relevant existing pages that could link to it. Also check whether the new page should link back to useful foundational content.

Internal Linking Best Practices

  • Prioritize relevance and reader usefulness over link quantity.
  • Use descriptive, natural anchor text.
  • Link to important pages from relevant contextual locations.
  • Keep navigation clear and avoid excessive repetitive links.
  • Check links after URL changes and site migrations.
  • Make sure important pages can be reached through crawlable links.

Internal Links and Website Structure

A clear website structure groups related pages around meaningful topics. A central guide can link to detailed supporting resources, while those resources can link back when it helps users understand the broader topic. Plan these connections around the audience's needs rather than creating links solely to manipulate search signals.

Frequently asked questions

Internal Link Suggestions FAQ

What are internal links?

Internal links connect one page on a website to another page on the same website. They help visitors navigate related content and help search engines discover pages.

How do I choose good internal links?

Choose links that are relevant to the surrounding content and useful to readers. Use descriptive anchor text, link to the most appropriate destination and check that the target page works.

How many internal links should a page have?

There is no universal ideal number. Add internal links where they help readers understand a topic or navigate the site, and avoid excessive or repetitive links that make the page harder to use.

Do internal links guarantee higher rankings?

No. Useful internal links can support navigation and page discovery, but they do not guarantee higher rankings or traffic.
