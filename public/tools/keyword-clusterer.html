Use this free keyword clustering tool to group related SEO keywords into topic clusters based on relevance and search intent. Keyword clustering helps you organize keyword research, plan useful content and identify search terms that may be targeted on the same page.

What Is Keyword Clustering?

Keyword clustering is the process of organizing search terms by topic, relevance and intent. A cluster may include a primary keyword alongside closely related phrases, questions and variations that address the same subject.

For example, "keyword clustering tool", "free keyword clustering tool" and "keyword cluster tool" are closely related phrases. Whether they should target one page should be assessed against the search results and the needs behind each query.

Why Is Keyword Clustering Important for SEO?

Keyword clustering can make content planning more systematic and help identify overlapping topics. It can also help distinguish between related searches that belong together and searches that deserve separate pages.

  • Organize related keywords into logical topic groups.
  • Identify primary and supporting keyword themes.
  • Plan content around user search intent.
  • Spot potential overlap between website pages.
  • Discover related questions and subtopics to cover.

Keyword Clusters and Search Intent

Search intent matters when deciding which keywords belong together. Informational searches may need guides, while commercial or transactional searches may need comparison or product pages. Do not combine terms just because they share words if users are looking for different outcomes.

What Makes a Good Keyword Cluster?

A useful cluster contains terms that share a topic and a similar purpose. Consider the audience, the type of page users expect, and the current search results before deciding whether a group should be served by one page or several.

Turn Keyword Clusters into a Content Plan

Choose a primary topic for each page, then use relevant supporting terms to guide headings, examples and useful subtopics. Review existing pages first to avoid publishing multiple pages that compete for the same search intent.
